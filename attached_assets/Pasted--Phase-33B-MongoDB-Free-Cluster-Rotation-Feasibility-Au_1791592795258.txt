# Phase 33B — MongoDB Free Cluster Rotation Feasibility Audit

## Objective

Review the existing Thanarah MongoDB deployment decision and document the feasibility of a free-cluster rotation and archival strategy for the production SaaS architecture.

This phase is strictly **documentation-only and read-only**.

## Business requirement

Thanarah needs tenant-isolated healthcare storage while minimizing recurring infrastructure costs.

The proposed idea is:

1. Use a MongoDB free cluster for active tenant workloads.
2. Monitor capacity and operational limits.
3. When a cluster approaches its permitted capacity, provision another eligible cluster through an officially supported process.
4. Verify the destination cluster, required indexes, tenant isolation, and migrated data before switching writes.
5. Retain the previous cluster as a read-only archive when the provider and plan permit it.
6. Keep an authoritative registry mapping each tenant's data to its actual storage location.
7. Never delete, overwrite, or silently abandon historical patient data.

Do not assume that unlimited free clusters, unlimited projects, repeated free-tier provisioning, or indefinite archival storage are available.

## Required investigation

Inspect the existing `TENANT_MONGODB_DEPLOYMENT_DECISION.md`, Phase 33 implementation, tenant storage registry, index provisioning, and related architecture documentation.

Use existing repository evidence and official MongoDB documentation to establish:

- Current Atlas Free cluster storage, database, collection, and operational limits.
- Restrictions on the number of free clusters per project and any relevant project or organization limits.
- Whether multiple free clusters can be provisioned under the intended account structure.
- Whether repeated provisioning or account/project rotation is permitted under current provider terms.
- Supported methods for migrating data between clusters.
- Whether the proposed archival cluster can remain available for read-only access and under what limitations.
- What happens if a new free cluster cannot be provisioned.
- How unique patient indexes and duplicate-protection guarantees behave on the destination.
- Whether the existing index strategy remains valid for the actual deployment topology.
- The impact of migration on write consistency, downtime, concurrent updates, tenant isolation, and auditability.

Cite official source URLs and record the date checked. Clearly separate verified provider facts from assumptions and unresolved questions.

## Architecture constraints

- Tenant healthcare records must remain in tenant-isolated MongoDB storage.
- Central MySQL/MariaDB remains the platform control plane; do not move patient records into it.
- One tenant may eventually use multiple physical databases or storage locations.
- Do not introduce automatic sharding, automatic migration, or rebalancing in this phase.
- Do not treat hostname, client-provided tenant IDs, or client-provided database names as authorization.
- Do not activate a destination before required indexes and tenant isolation have been verified.
- Do not promise zero downtime unless the migration design and tests demonstrate it.
- Do not assume the free tier meets production backup, recovery, availability, or healthcare data retention requirements.
